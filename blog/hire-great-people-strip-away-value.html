Do Not Hire Great People and Then Strip Away Their Value

A company can recruit excellent people and still waste their value if the operating system does not let their expertise change how work gets done.

Heather MacKay-Mencheski | June 4, 2026 | 5 min read

Executive Summary

Great hires do not create value just by being present. Their value shows up when the company uses their judgment, listens to their experience, and gives them enough authority to improve the work. If the system blocks that, the business can lose the benefit of the hire long before the employee leaves.

Source live conversation: Heather MacKay-Mencheski on why great people stay and the leadership responsibility behind that outcome.

The Bait-and-Switch Problem

Companies often hire people because they want their experience, ideas, and standards. Then the person starts and discovers that every improvement is slowed down, every concern is minimized, and every decision is pulled back into the old way of operating.

That feels like a bait-and-switch. The company asked for leadership but created a system that only wanted compliance.

Value Gets Stripped Away in Small Ways

It happens when leaders ask for ideas but never close the loop. It happens when the new manager is responsible for outcomes but not allowed to make decisions. It happens when an experienced technical person points out a process risk and the organization treats it as negativity instead of intelligence.

Over time, capable people learn that their full value is not wanted. They still do the job, but the company is no longer getting the best of what it hired.

Protect the Value of the Hire

Name the Expertise

Be clear about what judgment, experience, or leadership the company expected this person to bring.

Give It a Place to Work

Connect that expertise to decision rights, meetings, improvement priorities, and operating rhythms.

Close the Loop

When the person raises an issue, explain what changed, what will not change, and why.

This Is Not About Letting Everyone Do Whatever They Want

Strong people still need standards. They need clear priorities, business context, and accountability. What they do not need is a system that asks them to bring expertise and then punishes them for using it.

The best operating systems create room for expertise inside clear boundaries. That is how leaders get the value of a great hire without creating chaos.

A great hire cannot create full value inside a system built to ignore their judgment.

What CEOs Should Watch For

Watch for employees who used to raise ideas and no longer do. Watch for leaders who call every challenge "resistance." Watch for repeated approval bottlenecks. Watch for key people who are accountable for outcomes but lack the authority to fix the blockers causing those outcomes.

Those are not personality issues. They are system issues.

The Bottom Line

Hiring great people is only the first step. Keeping them, and getting the value of their expertise, requires a leadership system that lets them contribute at the level they were hired for. Otherwise, the company pays for capability and operates as if it does not trust it.
