# Turnover Cost Calculator

## Purpose

Use this skill when a manufacturing, aerospace, construction, or industrial leader needs a planning-level estimate of what employee turnover may be costing the operation.

The result should connect turnover to production, margin, workforce stability, supervisor load, quality, safety, and missed delivery pressure. Do not make the output sound like generic HR software.

## Inputs To Collect

Ask only for the minimum information needed:

1. Industry: manufacturing, aerospace, construction, or industrial services.
2. Approximate employee count.
3. Current annual turnover rate, if known.
4. Number of critical-role departures in the last 12 months, if known.
5. Average salary or wage for the roles most affected, if known.
6. Whether the biggest pain is hourly/frontline turnover, supervisor turnover, technical/certified-role turnover, or leadership churn.

## Default Assumptions

If the user does not know a number, use a clearly labeled estimate and explain the assumption.

- Use 50% of annual pay as a conservative turnover-cost estimate for general frontline roles.
- Use 100% to 150% of annual pay for supervisors, senior operators, maintenance technicians, quality leads, certified technical roles, or other critical roles.
- If pay is unknown, ask whether the role is hourly/frontline, supervisor, or technical/certified, then state that the result is a rough planning estimate.
- Do not pretend the calculation is exact. Call it an estimate for leadership discussion.

## Calculation

Calculate:

1. Estimated annual exits.
2. Estimated cost per exit.
3. Estimated annual turnover cost.
4. Estimated savings if turnover improves by 10%, 20%, and 30%.
5. The operational areas most likely absorbing the cost.

## Output Format

Use this structure:

1. Your estimated annual turnover cost is: `$X`.
2. If you reduce turnover by 10%, you could protect about `$X`.
3. If you reduce turnover by 20%, you could protect about `$X`.
4. If you reduce turnover by 30%, you could protect about `$X`.
5. Where the cost is probably hiding:
   - overtime coverage
   - training and ramp-up time
   - supervisor overload
   - missed production or delivery pressure
   - quality, safety, or rework risk
6. Recommended next step: identify which roles are driving the cost and whether those roles are critical to production stability.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim guaranteed savings.
- Do not diagnose legal, compliance, union, or employment-law issues.
- Do not imply HM Pinnacle can reduce turnover by a fixed percentage without discovery.
- Do not ask for private employee names, protected-class details, or sensitive HR files.
- Keep the calculation anonymous and planning-level.
